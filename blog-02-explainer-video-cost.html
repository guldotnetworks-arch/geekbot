What Factors Affect the Price?

1. Video Length

Every additional 30 seconds of animation adds approximately $1,000–$3,000 to a professional production. The sweet spot of 60–90 seconds keeps costs manageable while delivering maximum impact. Most studios price per finished minute of video.

2. Animation Complexity

Simple, flat character animation costs significantly less than complex scenes with multiple characters, detailed backgrounds, and frame-by-frame action. The more movement on screen per second, the more hours of animator time required.

3. Script and Voiceover

Professional scriptwriting adds $300–$800. Voiceover talent ranges from $150 for freelancers to $500–$1,500 for professional studio recordings with multiple takes and direction.

4. Revision Rounds

Most studios include 2–3 revision rounds in their base price. Additional rounds can cost $150–$500 each, depending on the stage of production where changes are requested. Changes at the animation stage are the most expensive.

5. Music and Sound Design

Licensed stock music costs $50–$300 per track. Custom-composed music for a 90-second video runs $500–$2,000. Sound effects and audio mixing add another $200–$600.

Hidden Costs to Watch Out For

When comparing quotes from different explainer video companies, these are the line items that often aren't disclosed upfront:

  • Rush fees: Need your video in 2 weeks instead of 5? Expect a 25–50% surcharge on production costs.
  • Extra revision fees: Some studios charge per revision after the included rounds — always ask upfront how many rounds are included.
  • Source files: Many studios charge an additional $500–$2,000 to provide the original project files (After Effects, Illustrator, etc.). Always negotiate source file ownership upfront.
  • Music licensing: Perpetual music licenses for commercial use can cost significantly more than standard licenses. Confirm the license scope in writing.
  • Multiple format exports: Web MP4, square format, vertical for Reels/TikTok, and broadcast formats may incur additional fees at some studios.
  • Script rewrites beyond scope: If your brief changes significantly after script approval, expect additional charges.

Smart Budget Tips for 2026

  1. Prioritize script over style. A brilliant script with mid-tier animation outperforms a mediocre script with premium visuals every time. Allocate at least 15% of your budget to professional scriptwriting.
  2. Start with 60–75 seconds. Don't try to explain everything in one video. A focused, shorter video delivers better results at lower cost.
  3. Negotiate source files upfront. Getting the project source files means you can make future edits cheaply without commissioning a full remake.
  4. Get 3–5 quotes before committing. The market varies dramatically. A consistent brief sent to multiple studios reveals the true price range for your specific project.
  5. Factor in distribution costs. A $5,000 video with a $2,000 promotional budget will outperform a $10,000 video with no promotion.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a 60-second explainer video cost?
A professional 60-second custom explainer video typically costs $4,000–$8,000 from a professional studio. Template-based 60-second videos cost $500–$1,500. Premium agency productions with 3D or complex character animation start at $10,000.
Can I get a good explainer video for under $2,000?
Yes, but with clear trade-offs. At this budget you're looking at template-based tools (like Vyond or Animaker), Fiverr freelancers with limited revision rounds, or very basic whiteboard animation. For a customer-facing homepage video, we recommend a minimum of $3,500 for professional quality.
Why do some explainer videos cost $25,000?
Premium-priced videos typically include: custom 3D modelling and rendering, broadcast-quality audio production, multiple languages/voiceovers, advanced visual effects, dedicated account management, and multiple rounds of strategic revisions with brand consultants.
Should I own the source files?
Yes — always negotiate for source file ownership. Without them, every future edit requires rehiring the original studio at full rates. At WREDD, source files are included in our project packages.
