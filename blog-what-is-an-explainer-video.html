What Is An Explainer Video?

An explainer video is a short, highly focused video — typically between 60 and 120 seconds — designed to communicate a business's product, service, or concept quickly and clearly. Using animation, motion graphics, storytelling, or live action, explainer videos break down complex ideas into simple, visually engaging narratives that audiences actually want to watch.

Unlike traditional advertisements that push a product, or long-form tutorials that overwhelm the viewer, explainer videos hit a precise sweet spot: they answer the question "What does this do and why should I care?" — in under two minutes.

"A well-crafted explainer video does in 90 seconds what a 2,000-word article takes 10 minutes to do — and it's remembered 6x longer."

— WREDD Animation Studio

The term "explainer video" first became mainstream in the early 2010s with brands like Dropbox proving that a single 90-second animation could generate millions in revenue. Today, explainer videos are a cornerstone of modern digital marketing, SEO strategy, and brand storytelling.

How Explainer Videos Work: The Psychology

Explainer videos aren't just pretty animations — they leverage deep psychological principles that make information stick. Understanding why they work is what separates average videos from ones that actually convert.

The Dual-Coding Theory

When your audience simultaneously hears a narration and sees matching visuals, their brain encodes the information in two separate channels — verbal and visual. This dual-coding dramatically increases recall. Studies show people remember 65% of visual information three days later, compared to just 10% of text.

Storytelling Triggers Emotion

Every great explainer video follows a proven narrative arc: Problem → Agitation → Solution → Result. By introducing a relatable character with a familiar pain point, then presenting your product as the resolution, you create an emotional investment that makes viewers far more likely to act.

The Attention Window

The average online viewer decides whether to keep watching within the first 8 seconds. This is why professional explainer video production always front-loads the problem statement — hooking the viewer before the solution is even revealed.

Key Insight: The most effective explainer videos aren't about your product — they're about your customer's problem. Lead with their pain, and your solution becomes the hero of the story.

Best Practices for Explainer Videos in 2026

The explainer video landscape has evolved dramatically. With AI-assisted production tools, shorter attention spans, and increasingly sophisticated audiences, here's what separates high-performing videos from forgettable ones in 2026:

  1. Lead with the problem in the first 5 seconds. Don't open with your logo or company history. State the pain point immediately — that's what keeps viewers watching.
  2. Keep it under 90 seconds. Data consistently shows engagement drops sharply after 90 seconds. Say more with less. Every frame must earn its place.
  3. Write for the ear, not the eye. Your script should sound natural when spoken aloud. Avoid jargon, passive voice, and long sentences.
  4. Use a human voiceover, not text-to-speech. In 2026, audiences can immediately identify AI voices. A real human voice builds exponentially more trust and emotional connection.
  5. Match your animation style to your brand personality. A law firm and a gaming startup need completely different visual languages. Style consistency across your brand is non-negotiable.
  6. Include a single, clear call-to-action. One CTA, clearly stated at the end. "Start your free trial," "Book a demo," or "Get a quote" — pick one and commit to it.
  7. Optimize for silent viewing. Add subtitles and animated text reinforcements. Over 85% of social media video is watched without sound on the first view.
  8. A/B test your thumbnail. Your video thumbnail determines whether anyone clicks. Test multiple options with a small ad budget before scaling distribution.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an explainer video exactly?
An explainer video is a short animated or live-action video — typically 60 to 120 seconds — designed to clearly communicate a product, service, or concept to a target audience. They use storytelling, visuals, and voiceover to answer the question "What does this do and why do I need it?" in the most engaging way possible.
How long should an explainer video be?
The ideal length for an explainer video is 60 to 90 seconds. Studies show that videos under 2 minutes retain the most viewers. For complex SaaS or product demos, up to 2 minutes is acceptable. Homepage explainers should aim for 60–75 seconds to maximize completion rates.
How much does an explainer video cost in 2026?
Explainer video costs in 2026 range from $500 for template-based animations to $25,000+ for fully custom premium productions. Most professional quality custom explainer videos from established studios fall between $4,000 and $10,000. The right investment depends on your goals, audience, and the complexity of your message.
What is the best type of explainer video for a SaaS company?
For SaaS companies, 2D character animation and motion graphics are the most effective explainer video styles. 2D animation allows you to show the software's benefits through relatable characters, while motion graphics excel at presenting dashboards, data flows, and technical workflows in a clean, professional visual style.
Do explainer videos really improve SEO?
Yes — significantly. Pages with video see an average 157% increase in organic traffic from search engines. Videos increase dwell time (time on page), reduce bounce rates, and earn more backlinks. YouTube videos also rank independently in Google search results, giving you two opportunities to appear on the first page for the same keyword.
How long does it take to produce an explainer video?
A professional custom explainer video typically takes 4 to 6 weeks from kickoff to final delivery. This includes script writing (3–5 days), storyboarding (5–7 days), design and style frames (5–7 days), animation (10–15 days), and voiceover/sound design (3–5 days). Rush production is available for an additional fee.
