<h2 class="wb-h2">The Startup <span>Communication Problem</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>

<p class="wb-p">There are three communication challenges unique to startups that an explainer video directly solves. Understanding them makes the ROI case obvious.</p>

<ul class="wb-list">
  <li><strong>Complex ideas, unfamiliar audience:</strong> Startups by definition are doing something that hasn't been done before — or doing it differently. That means your potential customers, investors, and partners don't have a mental model for what you do yet. You can't borrow context from familiarity. You have to build it from zero, fast.</li>
  <li><strong>Small team, massive communication surface:</strong> You have a team of 3–15 people trying to communicate to thousands of potential customers, dozens of investors, multiple media contacts, and a hiring pipeline — simultaneously. Every person on the team explaining the product differently creates brand fragmentation. One great explainer video creates one source of truth.</li>
  <li><strong>No time to explain:</strong> Every startup founder knows the feeling: 30 seconds into explaining the product at a networking event, you've lost them. The human attention span in low-stakes discovery contexts is brutally short. Video compresses your entire value proposition into a format the brain can absorb without effort.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wb-h2">Explainer Video for <span>Fundraising Specifically</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>

<p class="wb-p">The fundraising use case deserves special attention because the stakes are the highest — and the return on a good video at this stage can be transformational. <strong>An investor who watches your explainer video before your first meeting is already half-sold on the problem and the product before you open your mouth.</strong></p>

<div class="wb-img-banner">
  <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1522202176988-66273c2fd55f?w=1024&q=80" alt="Startup pitch presentation with animated explainer video boosting investor interest" loading="lazy">
  <div class="wb-img-caption">The investor-facing explainer — your most powerful pre-meeting asset in the fundraising process</div>
</div>

<p class="wb-p">When building an explainer video for fundraising, the content priorities shift slightly from a customer-facing video. Investors care about:</p>

<ul class="wb-list">
  <li>The size and severity of the problem you're solving — make them feel the pain before presenting the solution.</li>
  <li>Why now — what has changed in the market, technology, or behavior that makes this the right moment for this company to exist.</li>
  <li>Why you — what gives your team the unique right to solve this problem better than anyone else.</li>
  <li>The evidence of traction — early numbers, customer quotes, or market validation that signals this isn't just a hypothesis.</li>
  <li>The scale of the opportunity — help investors understand the ceiling, not just the floor.</li>
</ul>

<div class="wb-quote">"The best investor explainer videos are not product demos. They are problem-first narratives that make the solution feel inevitable — and make the investor feel like they'd be missing out not to be part of it."</div>

<h2 class="wb-h2">What to Include in Your <span>Startup Explainer</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>

<div class="wb-steps">
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">01</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>The Problem (0–15 seconds)</h3>
      <p>Open with the problem your target audience experiences. Make it visceral, specific, and instantly recognizable. If your viewer doesn't say "that's me" within the first 10 seconds, you've already lost them. Avoid industry jargon at this stage — speak in human pain, not technical framing.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">02</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Your Solution (15–35 seconds)</h3>
      <p>Introduce your product as the resolution to the problem you just established. Keep it high-level here — don't dive into features yet. The goal is to create a moment of relief: "finally, someone solved this." A clear, confident voiceover line like "Introducing [Product] — the only platform that..." works exceptionally well.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">03</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>How It Works (35–60 seconds)</h3>
      <p>Show — don't just tell — how the product works. For SaaS, animated UI walkthroughs. For physical products, animated demonstrations. For services, a stylized process flow. Keep this section to 2–3 core benefits or steps. More than three and retention drops sharply.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">04</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Social Proof (60–75 seconds)</h3>
      <p>A single data point or customer quote at this stage dramatically increases conversion. "Join 2,000+ teams who..." or "Used by companies like..." creates the validation signal that removes the final psychological barrier to action.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">05</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Clear CTA (75–90 seconds)</h3>
      <p>End with a single, unambiguous call to action. One CTA. Not two, not three — one. "Start your free trial." "Book a demo." "Join the waitlist." The viewer has just invested 90 seconds with you. Tell them exactly what to do next and make it frictionless.</p>
    </div>

<h2 class="wb-h2">Frequently Asked <span>Questions</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>

<div class="wb-faq">
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">When is the right time for a startup to invest in an explainer video? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">The right time is when you have a validated product and a clear target audience — typically post-MVP, pre-Series A. Pre-launch waitlist pages also benefit enormously from a short explainer. The wrong time is pre-validation, before you know what problem you're actually solving and for whom.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">How much should a startup budget for their first explainer video? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">For a first professional explainer, budget between $3,000–$8,000 for quality 2D animation from a reputable studio. This is the range where you get professional character design, quality voiceover, and full sound design without enterprise-level pricing. Think of it as the cost of one decent sales hire's first month — but this asset works 24/7 for years.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">Should a startup video be focused on the customer or the investor? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">Most startups need two slightly different versions. The customer-facing version leads with the user's problem and focuses on emotional resolution. The investor-facing version emphasizes market size, traction, and team right alongside the product narrative. Many startups produce one excellent customer video and simply add investor-specific context in a separate 30-second section or companion slide.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">What's the ideal length for a startup explainer video? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">60–90 seconds for a homepage or investor-facing explainer. Under 60 seconds for social and ad variants. Under 30 seconds for paid social. The rule is: use exactly as much time as you need to tell the story — not a second more. Most startup teams overestimate how much explanation their audience needs.</div>
  </div>

    <div class="wb-faq-q">Can WREDD work with early-stage startups with limited budgets? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>

We work within realistic budgets without compromising on the quality that matters most — script, voiceover, and core animation. We also offer phased production agreements for startups who need to deploy capital carefully as they scale.</div>
