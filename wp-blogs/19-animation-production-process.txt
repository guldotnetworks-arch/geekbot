<p class="wb-p">Most clients commissioning animation for the first time have the same experience: they know the outcome they want but have no visibility into how it gets created. Understanding the production process — what happens at each stage, what you need to provide, and where decisions become expensive to change — transforms a stressful experience into a collaborative one.</p>

<p class="wb-p">This guide walks through every stage of professional animation production from first contact to final delivery, with honest guidance on client responsibilities and the timeline decisions that most commonly determine whether a project ends on time and on budget.</p>

<h2 class="wb-h2">The 8-Stage <span>Professional Animation Production Workflow</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>

<div class="wb-steps">
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">01</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Discovery &amp; Strategy — 2–3 Days</h3>
      <p>The studio conducts a discovery call or questionnaire to understand your objective, audience, key message, tone, and existing brand assets. A creative brief is produced and agreed upon. This stage prevents misalignment that would be expensive to correct later. Client deliverable: approved creative brief. Most common skip mistake: clients who bypass this stage consistently encounter scope issues mid-production.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">02</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Script Writing — 3–5 Days</h3>
      <p>The scriptwriter produces a draft based on the agreed creative brief. For a 60-second animation, this is approximately 130–150 words of voiceover script plus scene direction notes. Clients review and provide feedback; typically two revision rounds are included. This is the cheapest and most important stage to get right — changes at script stage cost almost nothing. Changes at animation stage cost 5–10× more and delay delivery by days or weeks.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">03</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Voiceover Recording — 2–3 Days</h3>
      <p>Once the script is approved, professional voiceover is recorded. The studio presents voice options — typically 3–5 voice talent selections — for client approval. After talent selection, recording and initial edit take 1–2 days. The voiceover recording becomes the timing backbone for all subsequent animation work, making script approval before this stage non-negotiable.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">04</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Storyboarding — 4–6 Days</h3>
      <p>The storyboard maps every scene of the animation as static sketches, showing what will be on screen at each moment of the voiceover. Think of it as a comic-strip version of the final video. Client approval of the storyboard confirms the narrative structure, scene sequence, and visual direction before any animated frames are created. Requesting scene additions or structural changes after storyboard approval is the most common cause of timeline overruns.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">05</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Visual Design &amp; Style Frames — 5–7 Days</h3>
      <p>The art director creates 3–5 fully designed static frames showing the visual style: color palette, character design, typography, illustration style, and overall aesthetic. This is the visual identity of your animation. Client approval here locks the design direction. Changes to visual style after this stage require rework of all completed assets.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">06</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Animation — 10–15 Days</h3>
      <p>All approved design assets are animated against the approved storyboard and voiceover. This is the most time-intensive stage and the most expensive to revise. Animators work scene by scene, typically delivering a work-in-progress (WIP) edit at 50% and 90% completion for client review. Feedback at WIP stages should focus on pacing, flow, and critical corrections — not script changes or visual style revisions, which at this stage incur full rework cost.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">07</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Sound Design &amp; Music — 2–3 Days</h3>
      <p>The final animated cut receives a full sound design pass: background music selection or composition, sound effects, audio mixing, and voiceover equalization. Music licensing is handled by the studio, typically from a licensed library. Client approval of the final audio mix is the last creative approval before final render.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">08</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>Review &amp; Final Delivery — 2–4 Days</h3>
      <p>The finished animation is rendered in full quality and delivered for final client review. Minor timing or audio adjustments are handled in this stage. Final delivery includes all contracted file formats: typically MP4 (H.264) for web, MOV for broadcast, and any platform-specific exports agreed in the brief. Source files are provided if contracted. The project is closed and archived.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<h2 class="wb-h2">Your Responsibilities <span>as a Client at Each Stage</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>
<p class="wb-p">The most common misconception first-time animation clients have is that commissioning a studio means handing over responsibility entirely. In reality, a successful animation requires active client participation at defined checkpoints. Here is exactly what you are responsible for at each stage.</p>

<div class="wb-split">
  <div class="wb-split-visual">
    <div class="wb-split-grid"></div>
    <div class="wb-split-icon">
      <div class="wb-split-icon-box">✅</div>
      <div class="wb-split-label">Your Role</div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-split-content">
    <h3>Client Responsibilities by Stage</h3>
    <ul class="wb-list">
      <li><strong>Discovery:</strong> Provide a clear brief, brand assets, and reference examples. Assign one decision-maker to own approvals</li>
      <li><strong>Script:</strong> Review and approve within 48 hours. Consolidate all internal feedback before sending — multiple contradictory rounds delay production</li>
      <li><strong>Voiceover:</strong> Select voice talent from options provided. Approve recorded audio promptly</li>
      <li><strong>Storyboard:</strong> Review scene structure carefully — this is your last free opportunity to request sequence changes</li>
      <li><strong>Style frames:</strong> Approve visual direction decisively. Changing style after this stage has significant cost implications</li>
      <li><strong>Animation WIP:</strong> Focus feedback on pacing and flow. Avoid reopening script or style decisions</li>
      <li><strong>Final review:</strong> Confirm all contracted deliverables are received and technically correct for your use case</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<h2 class="wb-h2">Timeline Killers — <span>What Delays Projects</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>
<p class="wb-p">94% of animation project delays trace back to client-side issues, not studio issues. Understanding the patterns that cause delays helps you avoid them before they affect your launch date.</p>

<ul class="wb-list">
  <li><strong>Slow approvals:</strong> The single biggest delay factor. Each approval stage that takes 5 days instead of 48 hours adds 1–2 weeks to the total timeline</li>
  <li><strong>Approval by committee:</strong> Multiple stakeholders providing contradictory feedback stalls production and requires rework. Assign one approver</li>
  <li><strong>Script changes after voiceover recording:</strong> Requires re-recording and re-animating — typically adds 5–10 business days to the timeline</li>
  <li><strong>Style changes after style frame approval:</strong> All completed assets must be reworked — typically adds 7–14 days</li>
  <li><strong>Unclear or changing briefs:</strong> Scope creep mid-production is both costly and disruptive — time invested in a clear upfront brief pays back in full</li>
  <li><strong>Missing assets:</strong> Logo files, brand guidelines, product screenshots, or legal copy not provided at brief stage causes stalls throughout production</li>
</ul>

<div class="wb-quote">The animation is the easy part. The process discipline is what determines whether a project finishes on time, on budget, and as good as it can be.</div>

<h2 class="wb-h2">Your Pre-Signing <span>Checklist</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>
<p class="wb-p">Before signing with any animation studio, confirm the following to avoid the most common post-signing surprises.</p>

<ul class="wb-list">
  <li>Number of script revision rounds included in the quoted price</li>
  <li>Number of animation revision rounds included — and the cost of additional rounds</li>
  <li>Whether voiceover talent and licensing are included or billed separately</li>
  <li>Whether background music licensing is included or billed separately</li>
  <li>The file formats included in delivery — and whether source files are provided</li>
  <li>Ownership of completed animation — confirm you receive full IP ownership on final payment</li>
  <li>What happens if deadlines are missed — does the contract include any delivery guarantees?</li>
  <li>Point of contact and communication cadence throughout production</li>
  <li>Payment schedule — avoid studios requiring 100% payment upfront</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wb-h2">Frequently Asked <span>Questions</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>

<div class="wb-faq">
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">How long does a 60-second animation take to produce? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">A 60-second professional animation typically takes 3–5 weeks from creative brief approval to final delivery. This assumes a responsive client who provides approvals within 48 hours at each stage. If approvals are delayed, or if structural changes are requested after the storyboard or style frame stages, the timeline extends accordingly. Rush delivery options exist at most studios but typically incur a 20–35% premium on the base production cost.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">What do I need to provide to start an animation project? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">To start an animation project effectively, you should provide: your brand guidelines (or logo files and approved color palette at minimum), a clear written brief covering your objective, target audience, key message, and desired tone, any reference videos that represent the style you are looking for, any specific technical or legal copy that must appear in the video, and your delivery deadline. The more clearly you communicate your objective at the start, the less revision time is consumed later.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">Can I request changes during animation production? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">Yes, but the cost and timeline impact depend entirely on which stage the change is requested and what the change involves. Minor corrections — a name change, a color adjustment, a timing tweak — are typically absorbed within standard revision rounds. Structural changes — a new scene, a script revision, a style direction change — after those stages are approved carry rework costs and timeline implications. The golden rule: get every approval stage right the first time. That is what the approval process exists for.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">Do I own the animation once it is delivered? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">In any professionally structured contract, yes — full IP ownership transfers to the client upon final payment. Confirm this explicitly before signing. Some studios retain licensing rights that restrict how or where you can use the animation. At WREDD, clients receive full ownership of all delivered files upon final payment, with no usage restrictions. Source files can be included as an optional addition to the standard deliverable package.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">What is the most expensive mistake clients make during production? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">Requesting script changes after voiceover recording has begun. This requires the voiceover to be re-recorded, which delays the storyboard stage, which delays the animation stage — a cascade effect that typically adds 1–2 weeks to delivery and proportional cost. The fix is straightforward: treat script approval as the most important decision in the entire process. Do not approve the script until every stakeholder has signed off on every word. Changes at script stage cost nothing; changes later cost significantly.</div>
