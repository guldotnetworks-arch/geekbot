<h2 class="wb-h2">5 Questions to Ask <span>Every Animation Studio</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>
<p class="wb-p">These five questions reveal more about a studio's quality and reliability than any portfolio or sales pitch. A strong studio will answer all five with confidence and specificity. Vague, defensive, or avoidant answers to any of these are disqualifying.</p>

<div class="wb-steps">
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">01</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>"Can you walk me through your production process stage by stage?"</h3>
      <p>A professional studio should be able to describe every stage of their process — discovery, script, voiceover, storyboard, style frames, animation, sound design, delivery — with defined timelines and deliverables at each. If the answer is vague or skips stages, their process is ad hoc. Ad hoc processes produce inconsistent outcomes and are the primary cause of missed deadlines.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">02</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>"What is your revision policy, and what counts as a revision vs. a scope change?"</h3>
      <p>This question exposes whether the studio has a fair, clear contract structure. The answer should specify how many revision rounds are included at each stage, what constitutes a minor correction versus a structural change, and what the additional cost is for out-of-scope revisions. Ambiguity here leads to disputes. Clarity here protects both parties.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">03</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>"What percentage of your clients commission a second project with you?"</h3>
      <p>Repeat client rate is the single most honest quality metric in the animation industry. It is not manipulable by marketing — clients either come back or they don't. A healthy professional studio should have a repeat client rate above 60%. High-performing studios often report 70–80%+. If a studio cannot or will not answer this question, treat that as a red flag.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">04</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>"Can you provide a reference from a client in my industry or with a similar project type?"</h3>
      <p>A studio confident in their work will provide references without hesitation. Speaking to a previous client — even a 10-minute call — reveals the reality of working with the studio: communication quality, how they handle problems, and whether the final product matched the promise. Inability to provide references at all should end the evaluation.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-step">
    <div class="wb-step-num">05</div>
    <div class="wb-step-content">
      <h3>"What file formats and IP rights do I receive on final delivery?"</h3>
      <p>You should receive: full IP ownership of all delivered assets, the final render in all formats you need (MP4, MOV, platform-specific), and source files if required. Some studios retain IP or charge extra for source files without disclosing this in the initial quote. Get the complete deliverables list in writing before signing.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<h2 class="wb-h2">How to Evaluate <span>a Studio's Portfolio</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>
<p class="wb-p">A portfolio should be evaluated critically, not passively. Here is what to look for beyond whether the videos "look good."</p>

<ul class="wb-list">
  <li><strong>Range:</strong> Does the portfolio show multiple styles — 2D, motion graphics, 3D, whiteboard — or is every piece visually similar? Range indicates genuine capability; similarity suggests template dependency</li>
  <li><strong>Industry relevance:</strong> Have they produced content for businesses similar to yours? Industry-specific context understanding reduces briefing time and improves output quality</li>
  <li><strong>Script quality:</strong> Turn the sound off and read the subtitles. Is the script sharp, benefit-focused, and concise — or generic and feature-list driven?</li>
  <li><strong>Animation quality:</strong> Look for smooth, intentional motion — not jerky or template-standard timing. Easing, timing, and choreography are the marks of genuine animation skill</li>
  <li><strong>Sound design:</strong> Turn the sound on and listen. Does the music fit the content? Are sound effects present and purposeful? Poor audio is a consistent indicator of corners cut in production</li>
  <li><strong>Recency:</strong> A portfolio with work from 3+ years ago may not reflect current capability — studios evolve both positively and negatively. Ask for recent work if the portfolio is dated</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wb-h2">Your Quote <span>Checklist</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>
<p class="wb-p">Before accepting any studio quote, confirm that the following items are explicitly included or excluded with their pricing.</p>

<ul class="wb-list">
  <li>Script writing — included or billed separately?</li>
  <li>Number of script revision rounds included</li>
  <li>Professional voiceover talent — included or billed separately?</li>
  <li>Voiceover recording and editing — included?</li>
  <li>Background music licensing — included or billed separately?</li>
  <li>Sound effects and audio mix — included?</li>
  <li>Number of animation revision rounds included</li>
  <li>Final delivery file formats — which formats are included?</li>
  <li>Source files — included or available at additional cost?</li>
  <li>Full IP ownership transfer — explicitly confirmed in contract?</li>
  <li>Rush fee structure — what premium applies if timeline accelerates?</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wb-h2">Frequently Asked <span>Questions</span></h2>
<div class="wb-rule"></div>

<div class="wb-faq">
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">Should I choose a local animation studio or a global one? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">In 2026, geography is almost entirely irrelevant to animation quality. Professional animation production is fully remote-compatible — files, reviews, and approvals all happen digitally. The most important factors are quality of work, transparency of process, and communication reliability — none of which are geography-dependent. Limiting your search to local studios dramatically reduces your access to the best work at the best value. WREDD delivers world-class animation from Pakistan to clients on five continents with zero quality compromise.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">How do I know if an animation studio's portfolio is genuinely their own work? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">Ask to speak with the client featured in a specific portfolio piece. A studio producing genuine work will have no hesitation providing a reference for specific projects. You can also ask the studio to walk you through the production process of a portfolio piece — a team that made the work can explain exactly how and why every creative decision was made. Studios presenting others' work as their own cannot do this credibly. If they hesitate, the work may not be theirs.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">What is the difference between a freelancer and an animation studio? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">A freelancer is an individual artist — typically strong in one discipline (design, animation, or sound) but rarely all three at professional level simultaneously. A studio is a team with specialists at each production stage: strategist, scriptwriter, illustrator, animator, sound designer, and project manager. Studios also provide continuity — if one team member is unavailable, production continues. For professional business animation that requires strategic thinking, full production capability, and reliable delivery, a specialist studio consistently outperforms individual freelancers.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">How important is price when choosing an animation studio? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">Price should be a comparability check, not a selection criterion. First, establish that you are comparing equivalent scope — many low quotes exclude voiceover, music licensing, or source files that competitors include. Then compare within a reasonable market range. Price below market rate (more than 30–40% lower than comparable studios) is almost always a predictor of quality issues, not a value opportunity. The cost of a failed project — including time, rebriefing, and repurchasing — almost always exceeds the saving from choosing the cheapest option.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="wb-faq-item">
    <div class="wb-faq-q">What happens if I'm not happy with the final animation? <span class="wb-faq-arrow">+</span></div>
    <div class="wb-faq-a">The answer to this question is determined by your contract, not by goodwill. Before signing, ensure your contract specifies: the number of revision rounds at each production stage, what constitutes an in-scope revision, and the resolution process if the final deliverable does not meet the agreed brief. A professionally written contract protects both client and studio. If a studio's contract has no revision policy, no scope definition, and no dispute resolution process, that is a serious red flag regardless of their portfolio quality.</div>
